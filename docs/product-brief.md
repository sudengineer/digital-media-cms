# Product Brief — Digital Media CMS

## 1. Product Vision
Enable digital media teams to create, govern, and publish high-quality content through one dependable editorial workspace.

## 2. Problem Statement
Editorial work spans multiple roles and handoffs. When content status, metadata, approvals, and publishing schedules are managed across disconnected tools, teams experience:
- Unclear ownership and status visibility.
- Repeated data entry and inconsistent metadata.
- Delays caused by manual review coordination.
- Accidental publication or unauthorized changes.
- Limited auditability when content is corrected or withdrawn.

These are problem hypotheses for this fictional case. A real discovery phase would validate their frequency, severity, and business impact through interviews, workflow observation, and baseline data.

## 3. Product Opportunity
A centralized CMS can make the content lifecycle explicit and measurable. The opportunity is not simply to replace an editor; it is to reduce coordination cost and make publishing safer and more predictable.

## 4. Strategic Principles
1. **Editorial control:** Publishing is an intentional, permission-controlled action.
2. **Clarity over complexity:** Users should always understand content status, owner, and next action.
3. **Metadata at the source:** Capture structured metadata during authoring, not as an afterthought.
4. **Auditability by design:** Important actions must be attributable and traceable.
5. **API-first integration:** Publishing channels should consume stable, documented content contracts.
6. **Progressive delivery:** Release the smallest coherent workflow before expanding content types and automation.

## 5. Goals and Non-Goals
### Goals
- Establish one source of truth for editorial content and workflow state.
- Support draft-to-publication lifecycle for priority content types.
- Enforce role-based permissions and approval rules.
- Support scheduled publication and controlled corrections.
- Provide operational visibility and measurable product outcomes.

### Non-goals for MVP
- Advanced AI-assisted writing.
- Multi-brand white-label configuration.
- Automated translation.
- Complex campaign orchestration.
- Audience personalization.

## 6. Primary Hypotheses
| ID | Hypothesis | Validation method |
|---|---|---|
| H1 | A shared workflow reduces time spent coordinating approvals. | Compare baseline and post-release median review duration |
| H2 | Metadata validation at authoring reduces incomplete submissions. | Track first-pass completeness rate |
| H3 | A visible schedule reduces missed publication windows. | Track on-time publication rate |
| H4 | Role-based controls reduce unauthorized workflow actions. | Review permission-denial and audit events |

## 7. Success Definition
The MVP is successful if priority editorial teams can create, review, schedule, publish, and correct content without relying on a parallel manual status tracker, while meeting agreed security, reliability, and usability acceptance criteria.

## 8. Discovery Questions
- Which content types and channels are essential for the first release?
- Which roles can submit, review, approve, schedule, publish, and unpublish?
- Are approvals sequential, parallel, or conditional by content type?
- What constitutes a publishable content item?
- How should urgent corrections and takedowns be handled?
- Which systems own media assets, identity, analytics, and channel delivery?
- What are the expected peak publishing volumes and service-level objectives?
